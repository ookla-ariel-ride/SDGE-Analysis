Four behaviors drive the current bill, and the packages in §7 attack them in order of cost:

  • Large loads run during 4–9pm on-peak: 2,568 kWh/yr of >2.5 kW draw (EV top-ups, pool/laundry-class loads) lands in the most expensive window (60–87¢). Moving it past midnight turns ~$1,850 of cost into ~$325.
  • Charging spills past 6am: ~1,900 kWh/yr of high-power draw at 6–9am; past 6am weekdays it pays ~51¢ instead of 12.5¢. Finish charging by 6am.
  • Evening A/C through peak hours: summer evenings average 3–4 kW at 6–9pm. Pre-cooling in the 10am–2pm super-off-peak window (often on your own solar) and easing the setpoint 4–9pm shifts this; physically re-billing a 25% shift of on-peak house load is worth ≈ $480/yr (see §7 LOW; the weather regression in §9 attributes ~$230 of that to cooling specifically).
  • Midday exports earn little: since the 2026 TOU change, 10am–2pm weekday exports credit at only ~10.4¢. Self-consuming (laundry, dishwasher, EV top-up, pre-cool) beats exporting-then-reimporting at 51–87¢.

Dispatch policy: serve every import priced above the battery's stored-energy cost modeled

A stored kWh costs ~8.4¢ when it comes from midday solar surplus (the forgone export credit is only 7.6¢, ÷ 90% round-trip) or ~13.9¢ from a super-off-peak grid top-up. Against that, the tariff prices imports at 61–87¢ on-peak (4–9pm) and ~51¢ in every off-peak hour — weekday 6–10am, 2–4pm, and 9pm–midnight. So the economically correct policy is not "cover the evening peak": it's discharge against every import outside the super-off-peak windows, and the battery has the energy to do it — average midday surplus (~27 kWh/day) exceeds even the expanded pack's capacity, so morning discharge refills from the sun before the evening peak on most days. Only cloudy winter days bind.

One deliberate exclusion: intervals drawing ≥2.5 kW outside on-peak are EV-charging spillover, and the battery does not serve them. 8 · Array upgrades: more panels, bigger panels, or new microinverters? No, no, and not yet.

More panels: the numbers say no. The constraint isn't generation — it's timing. The array already exports 60% of what it makes (9,922 kWh/yr) because production peaks midday while the EV charges at night, and since the 2026 TOU change those midday exports credit at only ~10.4¢/kWh. A marginal new-panel kWh therefore earns ~10–12¢, while the marginal import it would offset (overnight super-off-peak) costs just 12.5¢ — nearly a wash. At typical retrofit pricing (~$2.50–3.00/W), added capacity returns roughly $170–190 per kW per year: a 13–16 year payback. A battery is the better use of the same sunlight — it monetizes those midday kWh at 60–87¢ instead of 10¢, and adds backup value.

The regulatory landmine matters more than the math. NEM 2.0 systems may only grow by ~10% or 1 kW without consequences. A larger addition gets billed under today's Solar Billing Plan (NBT), where exports earn avoided-cost rates (often 3–8¢), and clumsy paperwork can put your 2039 grandfathering at risk. Your NEM 2.0 status is one of this home's most valuable energy assets — don't disturb it for low-value midday kWh.

Repowering with higher-capacity panels: also no. That trade only makes sense for failing or badly shaded arrays. Yours produces 1,642 kWh/kW/yr with a rock-stable inverter-vs-meter ratio and no degradation signal at year 6 — you'd be discarding healthy hardware to make more of the energy you already can't use at full value.

Upgrading the microinverters because of clipping: the data says it isn't costing you much. The concern is reasonable: IQ7X units cap at ~315 VA (9.45 kW AC across 30), and Enlighten's per-panel clipping flags look alarming. The 5-minute data says otherwise. On May 11 — a 62.9 kWh peak-production spring day, i.e. maximum clipping risk (cool, clear, high irradiance) — true 5-minute power (PVOutput donation API) topped out at 8,520 W at solar noon against the ~9,450 W AC ceiling: a ~10% headroom cushion with no flat-line at the inverter limit. The annual data agrees: the 99.9th-percentile production hour is 8.82 kWh, and zero days show the flat-topped plateau sustained clipping produces. Whatever per-panel flags Enlighten raises are momentary and energetically trivial (≤1–2% of production, ≤~300 kWh). It's also midday energy worth only ~10.4¢/kWh, and the battery's charging needs are already covered many times over by the 27 kWh/day surplus. Net recoverable value ≤ ~$35/yr against ~$8,000–11,000 to swap 30 microinverters: a non-starter. (May 11's 62.9 kWh daily total is committed in data/pvoutput_daily.csv; its 5-minute curve comes from the PVOutput donation API, and the 8,520 W peak and 8.82 kWh 99.9th-percentile hour are in-session workpapers, not artifact-backed — the sample file data/pvoutput_5min_sample.csv shows a different day with the same no-clipping signature.) When would expansion make sense? If overnight consumption grows structurally — a second EV, electrified heating — and you've already installed storage to move midday energy into that demand; or when bidirectional EV charging (V2H) arrives in your ecosystem, at which point your car's 60–100 kWh pack dwarfs any wall battery and changes the whole calculus. Sequencing: a 2026-purchased PW3's 10-yr warranty ends ~2036 — three years before expiry — so an end-of-warranty replacement decision lands just as the battery's value steps up, a modest risk. One more asset fact: NEM 2.0 transfers with the property on sale, so the ~$1,772–2,268/yr above accrues to a buyer too. (data/extended_results.json → nbt_2039.)

Phantom baseload — decomposed measured

On the 44 nights with zero EV charging, the 1–5am import floor has median 1.025 kW (p10 0.785, p90 1.36) ≈ ~9,000 kWh/yr (~$1,800/yr at period-weighted prices). Three structural findings: it cycles (median within-night std 0.14 kW — a compressor-like duty-cycling component, e.g. fridge/freezer/pool equipment, not one flat load); it's seasonal — Sep–Oct nights run 1.37 kW vs 0.845 kW in May, a ~0.5 kW swing suggesting scheduled equipment with seasonal runtimes; and the lowest occupied-day import floor is 10.7–11.7 kWh/day. Resolution: the owner identifies the floor as home-lab computer systems (owner-attested) plus ordinary baseload — an intentional load, de-prioritized as a savings target for this household. The decomposition method (quiet-night floor, cycling signature, seasonal swing) remains the recommended first step for anyone whose floor is unidentified.

  • Grid data: SDG&E Green Button 15-min export (37,248 intervals, separate import/export channels); last 365 days analyzed.
  • Solar/load data: Enphase SAM 8760 hourly consumption (2025+2026 stitched), Enphase Site Energy Production daily report, PVOutput daily records — three production sources within ±2%, 0.9999 daily correlation; night-time residual of the derived series ≈ 0.
  • Rates: SDG&E Total Rates Tables eff. 6/1/2026 (EV-TOU-5, EV-TOU-2, TOU-DR1, TOU-DR2, TOU-DR-P, TOU-ELEC, DR); CEA Adopted Residential Rates eff. 6/1/2026 (Clean Impact); PCIA 2023 vintage $0.02828; WF-NBC/DWR-BC $0.00591; BSC $0.79343/day; Coastal baselines 9.0/9.2 kWh/day.
  • NEM 2.0: per-interval netting, exports at retail minus ~2.1¢ NBCs; cross-checked against monthly TOU-period netting (0.3% agreement — in-session workpaper). Grandfathering unaffected by plan changes or storage additions.
  • Battery model (integrated engine): three dispatch policies simulated per 15-min interval (analysis/battery_dispatch_policies.py, results in data/battery_dispatch_policies.json): evening-only ($1,708/yr for 1 × PW3), two-window (+6–9am house load; $1,946), and price-aware (every non-super-off-peak import; $2,325 — the published basis). In the 6.3% of intervals carrying both import and export, a discharge-window import is served rather than banking ~8¢ surplus. The battery modifies the physical import/export series and the modified year is re-billed with the canonical engine (analysis/rates.py: monthly per-period NEM netting, NBC on gross imports) — the same engine as the behavior model, so package figures come from ONE pipeline: EV shift first, then battery, re-billed end-to-end (MID battery marginal $2,245; no cross-model splicing). Charging is solar-surplus-first, then super-off-peak grid; 90% round-trip; 11.5 kW cap. EV-spillover intervals (≥2.5 kW outside on-peak) are never battery-served — the behavior fix moves that load for free. The §4 plan-matrix table is regenerated by analysis/battery_plan_matrix.py: the same price-aware dispatch trace billed under each top-3 plan's published table rates (data/battery_plan_matrix.json).
  • Behavior model: EV sessions detected as contiguous ≥30-min runs ≥2.5 kW above a rolling 24-h baseline with ≥8 kW peak excess; shifted energy physically added to same/next-day super-off-peak intervals under an 11.5 kW charger cap, then the modified year re-billed (energy conserved to the Wh; verified).
  • Package math: savings are deltas from analysis/behavior_rebuild.py — energy physically moved into destination super-off-peak intervals (11.5 kW charger cap respected, energy conserved to the Wh) and re-billed with the monthly per-TOU-period NEM netting model at current 6/1/2026 rates; absolute bills anchored to the $3,282 actual (§10). Paybacks are simple (no discounting, no rate escalation — SDG&E's recent high-single-digit annual increases would shorten them). No federal ITC (expired 2025); SGIP not assumed.
