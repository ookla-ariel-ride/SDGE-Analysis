Four behaviors drive the current bill, and the packages in §7 attack them in order of cost:

  • Large loads run during 4–9pm on-peak: 2,568 kWh/yr of >2.5 kW draw (EV top-ups, pool/laundry-class loads) lands in the most expensive window (60–87¢). Moving it past midnight turns ~$1,850 of cost into ~$325.

8 · Array upgrades: more panels, bigger panels, or new microinverters? No, no, and not yet.

More panels: the numbers say no. The constraint isn't generation — it's timing. The array already exports 60% of what it makes (9,922 kWh/yr) because production peaks midday while the EV charges at night, and since the 2026 TOU change those midday exports credit at only ~10.4¢/kWh. A marginal new-panel kWh therefore earns ~10–12¢, while the marginal import it would offset (overnight super off-peak) costs just 12.5¢ — nearly a wash. At typical retrofit pricing (~$2.50–3.00/W), added capacity returns roughly $170–190 per kW per year: a 13–16 year payback — worse than a battery, which at least monetizes the same sunlight at 60–87¢ instead of 10¢ (and adds backup value).

The regulatory landmine matters more than the math. NEM 2.0 systems may only grow by ~10% or 1 kW without consequences. A larger addition gets billed under today's Solar Billing Plan (NBT), where exports earn avoided-cost rates (often 3–8¢), and clumsy paperwork can put your 2039 grandfathering at risk. Your NEM 2.0 status is one of this home's most valuable energy assets — don't disturb it for low-value midday kWh.

Repowering with higher-capacity panels: also no. That trade only makes sense for failing or badly shaded arrays. Yours produces 1,642 kWh/kW/yr with a rock-stable inverter-vs-meter ratio and no degradation signal at year 6 — you'd be discarding healthy hardware to make more of the energy you already can't use at full value.

Upgrading the microinverters because of clipping: the data says it isn't costing you much. The concern is reasonable — IQ7X units cap at ~315 VA (9.45 kW AC across 30), and Enlighten's per-panel clipping flags look alarming. But the data settles it. Pulling true 5-minute power (PVOutput donation API) for May 11 — a 62.9 kWh peak-production spring day, i.e. maximum clipping risk (cool, clear, high irradiance) — the array topped out at 8,520 W at solar noon against the ~9,450 W AC ceiling: a ~10% headroom cushion with no flat-line at the inverter limit. Confirmed annually too — the 99.9th-percentile production hour is 8.82 kWh and zero days show the flat-topped plateau sustained clipping produces. Whatever per-panel flags Enlighten raises are momentary and energetically trivial (≤1–2% of production, ≤~300 kWh), and it's midday energy worth only ~10.4¢/kWh — the battery's charging needs are already covered many times over by the 27 kWh/day surplus. Net recoverable value ≤ ~$35/yr against ~$8–11k to swap 30 microinverters: a non-starter. The right time for IQ8s is when IQ7X units start failing — and yours are warrantied through January 2045, so failures get replaced free until then.

When would expansion make sense? If overnight consumption grows structurally — a second EV, electrified heating — and you've already installed storage to move midday energy into that demand; or when bidirectional EV charging (V2H) arrives in your ecosystem, at which point your car's 60–100 kWh pack dwarfs any wall battery and changes the whole calculus. Cheaper interim moves that soak up the 10¢ midday surplus: a heat-pump water heater on a 10am–2pm timer, midday EV top-ups on home days, and pre-cooling.

9 · Deeper analyses

Array health: 6-year degradation trend

PVOutput's size-normalized daily efficiency (kWh per kW installed) across full years 2021–2025 ran 4.749 → 4.839 → 4.245 → 4.528 → 4.501. A naïve fit reads ~1.3–1.7%/yr, but this metric isn't weather-normalized and the swing is dominated by weather (the wet 2023 El Niño winter sits mid-series and steepens the slope; 2025 generation is still within ~5% of 2021). A microinverter failure would show as an abrupt step-down or dead channel, not a gradual slope — and none appears. Verdict: normal aging, best-estimate ~0.5–1.0%/yr, no failing component. The array is performing as expected for its age. (The sharp drop in exported kWh in 2025 reflects higher self-consumption/load, not weaker generation.)

Inverter clipping (5-minute data): none worth acting on

Covered in §8 — peak power 8.52 kW vs the 9.45 kW AC ceiling on the year's best day. No upgrade warranted.

Weather-normalized cooling

Regressing daily non-EV load against local daily temperatures (Open-Meteo, coastal San Diego County) gives a base load of ~42 kWh/day plus 3.0 kWh per cooling-degree-day (base 65°F) — about 1,740 kWh/yr of A/C, of which ~780 kWh falls in the 4–9pm on-peak window. Pre-cooling in the 10am–2pm super-off-peak window (shifting ~half of on-peak cooling) is worth ≈ $230/yr; a modest efficiency/setpoint improvement another ≈ $100/yr. This quantifies the "evening A/C" behavior finding and lets you project bills for a hot vs. mild summer (each +100 cooling-degree-days ≈ +$60–90 without pre-cooling).

EV charging report card

The refined session detector (analysis/behavior_rebuild.py) finds 560 charging sessions over the year, 13,723 kWh — and your discipline is already ~81% good: 11,148 kWh lands in super-off-peak. The stragglers are what cost you: 878 kWh in on-peak and 1,697 kWh in daytime off-peak. Physically re-billing that energy in super-off-peak (monthly NEM netting, current rates) recovers $1,180/yr at 100% compliance ($1,004 at 80%) — and the decomposition is a surprise: only ~$550 comes from the 4–9pm window; ~$630 comes from daytime off-peak charging at ~$0.51–0.54/kWh. The fix is free: a schedule that starts at midnight, finishes by 6am, and avoids casual daytime plug-ins except 10am–2pm super-off-peak.

Plan wildcard: can TOU-DR-P + a battery beat EV-TOU-5?

No. TOU-DR-P offers lower base rates but adds a ~$1.16/kWh surcharge on ~15 called event days. EV-TOU-5 remains best in every combination tested.

Phantom / always-on load — flagged for a hands-on check

A naïve read of the 3–5am floor suggests ~1 kW always-on (~$1,800/yr), but that figure is almost certainly contaminated by legitimate baseload (refrigeration, pool/recirc pumps, water-heater standby) and imperfect EV subtraction — it is not $1,800 of recoverable "vampire" waste. Treat it as a prompt to spot-check with a plug meter on AV gear, a spare fridge/freezer, and pool equipment rather than a bankable saving. A realistic recoverable figure is more likely $100–300/yr.
